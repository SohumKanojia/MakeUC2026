City opens new accessible transit line

The city transit authority opened a new light rail line on Tuesday, the first in the network built to be step free along its entire length. Every platform on the twelve stop route meets the train floor at the same height, removing the gap that has long made the older lines hard to use with a wheelchair or a cane.

Tactile paving runs the full length of each platform, and every stop announcement is published to an open data feed that screen readers and braille displays can subscribe to directly. The authority said the feed was added after consultation with disabled riders, who asked for the information in text rather than only as audio on the platform.

Trains will run every eight minutes at peak times and every fifteen minutes in the evening. Fares match the existing network, and transfers from bus routes remain free within two hours.

Construction took four years and cost about three hundred million dollars, funded jointly by the city and a federal accessibility grant. A second phase, extending the line east toward the university, is scheduled to begin next spring.
